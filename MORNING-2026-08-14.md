### 4a. The machinery — when does a pick actually become scored?

🛑 **EVERYTHING IN §4a IS CONDITIONAL ON ONE UNVERIFIED VALUE — CHECK IT FIRST.**
The claim that `nflAutoScoreJob` is **armed** rests on
`system/config.nflAutoScore` reading `{enabled:true, dryRun:false}`, and that
was last observed on **2026-08-09**. It has not been re-read since, and I cannot
read it from a session machine. **If it has been disabled or returned to
dry-run, the scorer writes NOTHING and every "grades as games end" statement
below is false** — the kill switch is fail-safe, so a config read error also
means OFF.

**Thirty seconds, before you trust any of §4:** Firebase console → Firestore →
`system/config` → the `nflAutoScore` map. Expect `enabled: true` and
`dryRun: false`, both booleans.

What is NOT conditional on it: **the member's own Pick'em sheet colouring**
(§4b) runs entirely in the browser off `nfl_games` and does not involve the
scorer at all, so it is true either way. The scorer only gates the *totals* and
the *standings*.

Two jobs, both every 5 minutes; ingestion is unconditional, grading is armed
only if the config above says so:

| Job | Cadence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `syncNFLScoresJob` | `*/5` ([nflSchedule.ts:603](functions/src/nflSchedule.ts#L603)) | Pulls ESPN, writes live scores + status into `nfl_games`. A game reaches `FINAL` here |
| `nflAutoScoreJob` | `*/5` ([nflAutoScore.ts:659](functions/src/nflAutoScore.ts#L659)) | Grades every live pool — **only when `system/config.nflAutoScore` is `{enabled:true, dryRun:false}`**. ⚠️ carried from 2026-08-09, **not re-measured** |

**It grades per game as each game ends — not Tuesday.** This is the crux of your
question and the answer is the good one. Every mid-week pass runs *provisional*
(`provisional: !isWeekComplete(...)`, [nflAutoScore.ts:319](functions/src/nflAutoScore.ts#L319)),
and a provisional pass grades exactly the games that are **both** concluded
**and** past their own lock:

```ts
// functions/src/nflPools.ts:1132-1137
const revealed = (g) => isTerminalGame(g) && gameLockClosed(g);
const gradableGames = provisional ? games.filter(revealed) : games;
```

So the 1pm games are graded by ~4:20pm, and the pool's standings totals move
with them. **Worst case latency from a game ending to a member's total moving:
about 10 minutes** — up to 5 for ESPN's FINAL to land, up to 5 more for the
scorer's next pass.

Two honest caveats:

- A `FINAL` the feed reports **no scores** for does not count as concluded
  ([weekCompletion.ts](functions/src/lib/weekCompletion.ts) `isTerminalGame`).
  The scorer waits and self-heals on the next pass. Correct, but it means a
  broken payload shows as "not scored yet" rather than as an error.
- **Survivor and Margin are different by design.** Their per-week result is held
  until the **weekly lock has passed** (`weeklyPickReady`,
  [nflPools.ts:1149-1181](functions/src/nflPools.ts#L1149)). Publishing a
  survival mid-Sunday would reveal a member's pick while they could still change
  it, and a Margin 0 would flip when the game ends. So Survivor/Margin members
  see nothing move until the weekly deadline has passed.

> is just never rendered". **That is wrong, and wrong in the direction that
> costs time**: it reads as "an afternoon of UI work" when the data is not
> reachable by the client at all.
>
> Three separate doors are shut, and all three were shut on purpose:
>
> 1. **The standings projection strips it.** `sanitizeWeeklyResults`
>    ([nflScoringEngine.ts:698-708](functions/src/nflScoringEngine.ts#L698))
>    deletes the `games` and `game` keys from every week before writing
>    `pools/{id}/standings/current`. The `StandingsRow` interface is an
>    **allowlist**, not a filter — its own comment says per-game maps are
>    excluded because standings only need the summaries.
> 2. **Raw entries are unreadable by other members.** That is #414, live in
>    production since 2026-08-12.
> 3. **`getPoolPicks` returns revealed *picks*, not their graded *results*.**
>
> So building G2 means extending a server projection or the callable, **and
> making a fresh reveal-policy decision about it** — not adding a component. It
> is at minimum a functions deploy, and arguably its own plan.
>
> This raises my confidence in the "after kickoff" recommendation rather than
> lowering it: the cheap-looking version of G2 is the one that would have
> reintroduced a protected entry read.

**My recommendation: G1, and only G1, before launch.** It is a small frontend
change with no reveal risk, it removes the "why does one pool type feel dead"
reaction, and it needs no plan and no functions deploy. G2 is the more exciting
one and it is also the one where a mistake shows somebody a pick they should not
see — it deserves its own careful pass after kickoff, not a rushed one now.
