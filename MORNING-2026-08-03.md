## 2. #347 — the roster fix, and the finding that changed its design

`#344` stopped `setPaidStatus` minting a Member Record in any pool; `#338` stopped
an already-forged one being a *reminder target*. Neither deletes what was already
minted, and §4a covered targeting **only** — so `src/utils/poolRoster.ts` still
put every document in the members collection on the commissioner's roster, in
`memberCount`, and in the dues totals.

Three readers in that file walk the collection, so I routed all three through one
helper rather than fixing the visible one.

### 🔴 The finding worth reading

I first filtered on the canonical `joinedAt` stamp alone, matching #338. **codex
round 1 showed that also discards a real participant's un-stamped record** — and
those carry real money state:

- `setPaidStatus`'s **commissioner branch merges `paidStatus: 'PAID'` without
  stamping `joinedAt`**;
- its own comments record that `reconcilePaymentTruth` promotes claim-only
  documents to PAID from a paid entry.

The failure would have been silent and expensive: **you mark someone paid, the
roster keeps showing UNPAID, and `collected` loses the fee.** Hiding payment
truth in order to hide a forgery. I verified this against the source before
acting on it rather than taking the reviewer's word.

The fix uses `isProvableMember` — canonical record **OR** `participantIds` — which
is the rule `PLAN-SETPAIDSTATUS-MEMBERSHIP` §4 already endorses. The #344 exploit
wrote *only* the member document, so a forged record still has neither evidence
and still vanishes. That predicate **moved to `shared/memberRecord.ts`** (re-exported
from the functions-side file, so no importer changed): `src/` cannot import that
file because it pulls in `firebase-admin`, and re-deriving the two-evidence rule
client-side is the drift that hoisting the first predicate existed to prevent.

This door is deliberately **looser** than the reminder door, which still uses the
canonical half alone. #338 refuses `participantIds` there because it is
manager-writable and would make that callable an arbitrary-email primitive.
Nothing on the roster path sends anything — the consequence is a row on the
manager's own roster and a fee in their own dues total.

**One reader is deliberately NOT filtered**, and it is commented in place so it
does not read as an oversight: `PaymentsPanel`'s `myMember` is keyed on the
caller's own uid, so the only record it can return is their own.

## 3. #349 — the docs-state guard you asked for two sessions ago

The obvious rule — *heading date must not be older than the newest date in its
section* — was already measured as crying wolf on three real headings. **I
re-measured and it also fires on `PICKUP §0`**, whose section mentions
`2026-08-13`, the first 16-game slate: a future deadline, not a record. Two
independent false-positive classes, and this repo has twice written down that an
invariant which cries wolf gets ignored and then the real one is missed.

So the rule is narrowed on both sides: the **heading** must carry a state word
from a closed vocabulary, and the offending **content** date must sit on a line
that also carries one — same kind of fact, not merely a later number.

**Measured, and re-measured after every subsequent narrowing:**

- **0 fires** on the corpus as it stands;
- **exactly 1 fire** against `PICKUP-PRESEASON-PILOT.md` as it was at `3cfd968^` —
  the real defect — on the right heading, citing the right line.

The second case fails when two `MORNING-<same-date>*.md` files exist and neither
names the other in its first ten lines. Its vocabulary includes `continues`, not
only `supersedes`, because two of the four real same-date pairs are genuine
continuations; forcing "superseded" onto those would mean writing something false
to satisfy a test. Three historical docs gained an accurate banner, with the
creation order taken from `git log --diff-filter=A` rather than guessed from
filenames.
